**Cardi-Golf is a platform for running amateur golf tournaments among friends**: live scoring, side games, a Calcutta auction, money tracking and settlement. Any organizer can create a tournament, configure its rules, and run it from phones and a TV.

**Nacho's Bachelor Invitational (Los Cabos, 12 players) is the first tournament it will run.** It is the launch customer and the acceptance test, not the product. Sections 2 and 5–18 describe that tournament in full detail, because a concrete first customer is how you know the platform is complete; section 0.5 says how to read them so the result stays generic.

### 0.4 Shipping loop: branch → PR → watch → green → merge
1. Work on a `claude/<topic>` branch. `main` is what Vercel deploys to production, so never push to it directly.
2. Open a PR per milestone, or per coherent chunk. Then **subscribe to its activity** (`subscribe_pr_activity`) so CI failures and review comments wake you.
3. **Drive it to green yourself:**
   - A red `check` is your work now: find the root cause, fix, and push. "Flake" is not a root cause, and never skip or disable a test.
   - Address every review comment, or reply saying why not.
4. Merge once `check` is green, then send Diego (in Spanish):
   - The Vercel preview/production URL.
   - What to try.
   - What comes next.
5. If you can schedule a check-in (`send_later`), keep one armed about an hour out while a PR is open, and stop once it's merged.
   - **Previews are on demand** (2026-10-16). Vercel's Hobby plan builds at most 100 deployments a day, and a preview of every push to every `claude/**` and `dependabot/**` branch spent them twice in two days, refusing production's deploy of main for up to 24 hours. `vercel.json` (`git.deploymentEnabled`) builds no preview for those branches; CI never needed one. When Diego should try a branch before it merges, make one deployment of it through the REST API (`POST /v13/deployments` with `gitSource` and its ref) and send that URL; otherwise send production's after the merge. `src/lib/ops.test.ts` holds the rule.
6. **Don't clean up after a merge.** A merged PR unsubscribes the session by itself, and a check-in that fires after the merge just finds nothing to do. So never call `unsubscribe_pr_activity` or `delete_trigger` on the way out. The Claude app asks Diego before both, whatever `.claude/settings.json` says (2026-09-29).

### 0.5 Platform, not a one-off: how to read the rest of this brief
Everything from section 2 on is written for the first tournament. Build it so the *second* tournament needs zero code changes. Concretely:

- **A tournament is a row, not a deploy.** `tournaments` is the root of everything; every other table hangs off it (directly or through `rounds`/`players`). An organizer creates a tournament in the app, and one deployment hosts many. The "Ensayo" rehearsal (§13, §17) is simply another tournament.
- **Rules are settings, games are modules.** Every number in section 5 (entry fee, allowance, cap, cut parameters, prize amounts, Calcutta shares, snake payout, tiers) is a value in `tournaments.settings` (§18 shows the first tournament's values; they are that tournament's defaults, not constants). Each side game (individual Stableford, best round, pairs "Matrimonios", snake "La Víbora", fewest putts, Calcutta) is an **engine module** that a tournament turns on or off and parameterizes. A tournament with only individual Stableford and no Calcutta must work. Add game *formats* (e.g. gross stroke play, match play) only when a tournament needs them, but leave the module seam so they slot in.
- **Field size, days, tiers and tees are data.** Don't hard-code 12 players, 2 days, 4 tiers of 3, or 18 holes per round. Tiers are a list the organizer defines (names + which pair with which); a tournament may have none. Rounds are 1..N. Groups are 2–4 players. The pairs game defines its own pairing rule (A↔D, B↔C is the first tournament's).
- **Names are copy, not code.** "Matrimonios", "La Víbora", "La Cuchara de Palo", "Rey del Birdie" are the first tournament's labels for generic concepts (pairs game, snake, last place, most-birdies award). Each module has a default label the organizer can rename per tournament. Code identifiers stay generic (`pairsGame`, `snake`, `lastPlace`, `mostBirdies`). Copy that names a game takes its label (2026-10-16, STRAT-11): the snake's names its object on the Tarjeta, the boards, the feed, the awards («<label> de Oro»), the Comité and the Reglamento (`labelInSentence`: «Tiene la Víbora»), and the champion takes `labels.trophy` only when one is set. `src/screens/tournament/labels.test.tsx` renders every tab, the TV and each ceremony step under other names and fails on «Putter», «víbora», «Matrimonios», «Cuchara» or «novio».
- **Brand per tournament.** Logo, name, tagline, and an accent color are tournament fields, shown on Entrar, headers, TV mode and share cards. The design tokens in §14 are the *platform's* look; `assets/nacho-logo.png` is the first tournament's logo (seeded into it, not baked into the shell). The groom flag (§5.1) becomes a generic "honoree" spotlight the organizer may or may not set.
- **Organizers have accounts; players don't need one.** Organizers sign in with email (Supabase Auth, magic link or password) and can create tournaments. Players keep the frictionless flow: tap your face, enter your PIN, no account. A player is a row inside a tournament; the same human in two tournaments is two rows (fine for v1). Players join a tournament by link or 6-character code.
- **Money model is generic.** Entries, prizes, auction purchases, buybacks and payouts are all `payments` rows with a `kind`; the settlement (§11) works for any set of enabled modules and any banker.
- **One engine, many configurations.** `computeTournament(snapshot, settings)` reads the enabled modules from `settings` and returns state only for those. The section-6 tests run against the first tournament's settings; add at least one test per module that proves it can be *disabled* (state absent, money unaffected) and a "minimal tournament" test (8 players, 1 round, individual Stableford only, no tiers).
- **Copy stays Spanish (Mexico) in v1.** The platform is built for Diego's circle first; keep strings in one place (`src/i18n/es-MX.ts`) so a second language is a file, not a refactor. Don't build the second language.
- **Scope discipline.** Generic ≠ bigger. Build exactly the modules and screens the first tournament needs, but built on tournament-scoped data, settings-driven rules and renamable labels. No marketplace, no billing, no public discovery, no multi-org roles beyond organizer / player.
- **The launch scope is a list** (STRAT-01, STRAT-09, 2026-10-16). `docs/strategy/README.md` is the one-page strategy: who Polo is for, the north-star metric («torneos cerrados al peso»), the non-goals and the three bets, and it wins over this brief on scope. Areas built before the review that the trip doesn't need (Ronda rápida, social, crews, most of Admin de Polo) keep working but don't count as done for the launch. `docs/strategy/launch-scope.json` files every route and every migration under an area, each area in or out of the launch; `src/lib/launchScope.test.ts` fails on a route or migration it doesn't list, so a new one goes there, with its area, in the same change.

When a later section says "the 12 players", "Nacho", "A/B/C/D", "$2,500", read it as "this tournament's players / honoree / tiers / entry fee".

## 2. The first tournament: what it must do

Twelve friends are playing a 2-day golf tournament in Los Cabos for Nacho's bachelor trip. There is a $30,000 MXN prize pot, several side games, and a Calcutta auction with its own pot. The app replaces paper cards, spreadsheets, and arguments. (Read with §0.5: everything here is one tournament's configuration of the platform.)

**Users**
- The organizer, who creates the tournament and configures it from a phone or laptop.
- The 12 players, on their phones on the course: bright sun, patchy signal, one hand free, a few beers in.
- The Comité (organizers), who set things up and correct mistakes.
- A TV at the dinner and at the villa, showing the auction and live boards.

**Core principle: store raw facts, derive everything.** The database stores only tournaments and their settings, players, handicaps, courses, groups, pairs, strokes/putts/pick-ups per hole, snake tiebreak answers, auction lots/bids/ownership, payment marks, and overrides. **The database boundary is typed** (ARCH-03, 2026-10-17). `src/data/database.types.ts` is generated from the migrations (`scripts/gen-db-types.mjs`, Supabase's `Database` shape: Tables Row / Insert / Update / Relationships, Functions Args / Returns; two catalog-read differences, written in its header: a `tournament_id` that 0030's tenant trigger fills is optional on Insert, and a function's arguments take null and a jsonb one anything JSON), and never edited by hand: the `db` job fails when it is stale. `createClient<Database>` types every table read and write. A database function is called one way, `rpc(name, args)` (`src/data/rpc.ts`; `anonRpc` on a link to watch): the name and arguments are the generated ones, a refusal is an `ApiError` with Postgres's code (`isMissingFunction`: PGRST202, 42883), a client that can't be made is a rejected call. A jsonb answer has its shape in the function's body, so the caller names it (`.as<T>()`, only on jsonb or as a narrowing) or checks it (`.parse(schema)`, an `AnswerError` naming paths, never values). Checked with zod: `save_hole`'s answer (a malformed one is retried, never settled halfway), `my_membership`, `lookup_tournament`, `spectator_board`, and what the phone reads back from storage where a bad value would break a screen (the saved boards, «Tu último torneo», the link token's stash). A mapper takes its table's row by the generated column names (`RowOf<'scores'>`: a renamed column fails the typecheck; values stay as they come, since a link's board and a live change may leave columns out). A CHECK-constrained text column is `string` in the types: narrowing it to the engine's words is a cast with the constraint named. Tests: `src/data/rpc.test.ts`, `outbox.push.test.ts`, `snapshotCache.test.ts`, `session.test.ts`, `profiles.stash.test.ts`.

**Server state outside a tournament** (ARCH-18, 2026-10-17; `src/data/query.ts`, `src/components/useServerMutation.ts`). **One game seam** (ARCH-15, 2026-10-16; `src/engine/games/game.ts`). There were three, each with its own contract and an `any` at the boundary: the singletons (`modules/`), the instance games (`games/`) and the formats.
- **One contract, `Game<S, Ctx>`:** `compute(ctx)` → state, `prizes(state, ctx)`, `warnings?(state, ctx)`. A singleton (`GameModule`, `settings.modules[id]`: individual, best round, pairs, snake, fewest putts, the Calcutta) is a game a tournament has at most once; an instance game (`GameImpl`, `settings.games[]`) gets its config, entrants, rounds and pot in its context and also draws a `GameBoard`. Each game's money warnings are its own `warnings()` (the Calcutta's moved out of `computeTournament`); the pairs' group composition stays a field warning, before the pairs' own and never in «por asignar».
- **Formats stay the main event's ranking strategy** (`formats/`): the individual game ranks whatever its format gives it.
- **Typed registries.** `ALL_MODULES: ModuleRegistry` and `ALL_GAMES: GameRegistry` are mapped types over `ModuleStateMap` / `GameStateMap`, each implementation under its own id or type (`GameModule<S, K>`, `GameImpl.type` is its config's). `state.modules.snake` is a `SnakeState`; `state.games[id]` is a union per type (`GameResultState`, built through `GameConfigFor<K>`, TS's correlated unions) that `isGameOf(g, 'contest')` narrows, config and state together (`contestStateOf` for the Tarjeta and Comité › Juegos, which cast `state as ContestState`). No `any`, no cast; the one assertion left is a test's stand-in (`ComputeOptions.games`), whose state is the test's own.

## 5. The first tournament's rules: the source of truth for its configuration

The printed rules sheet the group received says the same thing. If you find a conflict, this section wins and you flag it to Diego. Every parameter here maps to a setting or a module option (§0.5, §18); every rule is implemented in the engine generically and *configured* to these values for this tournament.

### 5.1 Field
- 12 players, $2,500 MXN entry each, for an entry pot of $30,000.
- Four tiers of three players, assigned by the Comité by handicap: **A** (best), **B**, **C**, **D**.
- One player is Nacho, the groom. Flag him with `is_groom` and give him a special badge in the UI.
- Tier D plays the forward tees. The Comité accounts for that in the base handicap. The app still supports a tee per player, because par and stroke index can differ by tee.

### 5.2 Handicaps
- `baseHcp` is set by the Comité for each player: the average of their last 3–5 real rounds minus the course rating, capped at 54. It's entered in the admin and locked before the Calcutta (2026-10-13, migration 0046, UX-28: «Fijar hándicaps» in Comité › Jugadores, or the tournament leaving setup, fixes them; after that a change needs the Comité's reason and everyone sees it; see `players.handicap_note` in §7). One base handicap for the whole tournament even though the two days are on different courses (Solmar, Quivira); the Comité chose simplicity over a per-course slope adjustment. Make a per-round course adjustment possible as a setting (`handicap.perRoundSlope`, default off), but don't turn it on.
- Day 1 playing handicap: `PH1 = roundHalfUp(0.80 × min(baseHcp, 54))`. The group chose 80%; don't relitigate it.
- Strokes received on a hole: `floor(PH / 18) + (SI <= PH % 18 ? 1 : 0)`, where SI is the stroke index of that hole for the player's tee.
- A plus handicap (below 0) plays below 0 and gives its strokes back from the easiest hole up, as WHS allocates it (MONEY-21, 2026-10-16): index +1.2 on a par-rated tee is a course handicap of −1, at 100 % a playing handicap of +1 (written as golfers write it), one stroke back on SI 18. It used to stop at 0. The day 2 cut never moves one: it takes strokes away and never adds one, so `PH2 = PH1` for a plus handicap and `max(0, PH1 − cut)` otherwise. A Comité override is still 0 to 60 (`handicap_overrides`' check). The card draws a ring per stroke given back (`strokeMarks`), said «Da 1 golpe».
- **Anti-sandbag rule for Day 2.** Let `P1` be the player's Day 1 Stableford points. Then `cut = P1 > 36 ? min(4, floor((P1 − 36) / 2)) : 0`, and the Day 2 playing handicap is `PH2 = max(0, PH1 − cut)`.
  - The cut applies to every handicap game on Day 2: individual, Matrimonios, and best round.
  - Nobody ever gets strokes added.
  - The Comité can override a playing handicap; overrides require a reason and are audit-logged.
- The allowance (0.80), the cap (54), and the cut parameters (36, 2, 4) live in settings.
- How `baseHcp` is obtained per player (a WHS index, an estimate from three scores, or a number typed by the Comité) and how it becomes a course handicap for the tee he plays each day is §13b. The rules above start from that course handicap.

### 5.3 Individual Stableford (main event)
- Points per hole: `pickedUp ? 0 : max(0, par + strokesReceived − gross + 2)`. That gives 0 for net double bogey or worse, 1 for bogey, 2 for par, 3 for birdie, 4 for eagle, 5 for albatross.
- Picking up ("Levantar") scores 0 points. Players pick up once they can't score.
- Total over 36 holes.
- Payouts: 1st **$10,000**, 2nd **$5,000**, 3rd **$3,000**, 4th **$2,000**.
- **Tiebreak (countback):** Day 2 total, then Day 2 holes 10–18, then 13–18, then 16–18, then hole 18. If still tied, the tied players share the sum of the prizes for the places they occupy, split evenly.
- The champion also receives the physical trophy, the Putter, courtesy of the travel agency (Golfbreaks).
- Last place overall wins **La Cuchara de Palo**: a wooden-spoon trophy plus a Calcutta slot (5.9).

### 5.4 Mejor ronda del día (best round)
- **$1,200 each day** to the highest single-day Stableford total.
- Open to everyone; a player can win this and the main event.
- Day 2 uses the adjusted handicaps.
- Tie: countback within that day (holes 10–18, 13–18, 16–18, 18), then split.

### 5.5 Los Matrimonios (pairs game)
- Six fixed pairs: each A is paired with a D, and each B with a C.
- **The draw** happens at the end of the Calcutta dinner, and the app runs it (section 13). Nacho doesn't draw: he picks his partner from the tier he's matched with. Everyone else is drawn at random.
- **Pair score:** the sum of both partners' Stableford points on every hole, over 36 holes. Each partner uses his own handicap strokes.
- **Prizes:** 1st pair **$2,000** ($1,000 each), 2nd pair **$1,000** ($500 each).
- **Tie:** better combined Day 2, then split.
- **Pairs play together both days.** Each foursome is one A+D pair and one B+C pair.
  - Day 1 groups come from the draw: random A+D vs B+C matchups.
  - Day 2 groups are set by the pair standings, with the top two pairs in the last group. Default: rank pairs 1–6, then groups are (5,6), (3,4), (1,2) in tee-time order. The Comité can override, and the app warns if a group isn't one A+D pair plus one B+C pair.
- **Tarjeta cruzada:** each pair keeps the other pair's card (section 9.3).
- **Who signs is a setting** (2026-10-14, UX-23, migration 0054): `settings.cardSigning` is `pairs` (this tournament's: each pair signs the other pair's card, only with the pairs game on), `marker` (another player of his group signs each player's card, one signature per player per day) or `none`. A tournament stored without the key reads as it always behaved: `pairs` with the pairs game on, `none` without (`cardSigningMode` in `src/engine/core/cardSigning.ts`, `card_signing_mode` in SQL). The create wizard writes `pairs` with the pairs game and `marker` without; a Ronda rápida is created with `none`; Comité › Torneo › Reglas changes it («Quién firma las tarjetas», `pairs` offered only with the pairs game, and a stored `pairs` with the game off holds Guardar until changed: `settingsSense`'s `signingPairs`).

### 5.6 La Víbora (the snake), per group, per day
- Putts are strokes taken on the green, and the player enters them.
- In each group, the snake belongs to whoever most recently took **3 or more putts**. Process holes in the order the group plays them, which depends on its starting hole.
- If two or more players take 3+ putts on the same hole, the one who **holed out last** takes the snake. When this happens, the app asks "¿Quién embocó al último?" and stores the answer.
- **At the end of the round** (group finished 18 holes): the three players not holding the snake get **$200 each** from the pot. The holder gets $0 and carries the rubber snake on his bag until the next round.
- If nobody in the group 3-putted all day, the four split the $600 ($150 each).
- Total: 3 groups × 2 days × $600 = **$3,600**.

### 5.7 Menos putts (fewest putts)
- **$1,000** to the lowest total putts over 36 holes. Only strokes on the green count.
- Tie: split.
- Picked-up holes follow the setting in section 18.
- A hole saved with strokes and no putts (a restore of an older export, a fix by hand, an older build's direct write; the Tarjeta and the Comité always send putts) never reads as 0 putts (MONEY-18, `src/engine/core/putts.ts`). Menos putts leaves it out, so the player has fewer holes than the field and can't take the prize; the snake and a three-putt fine read it as no three-putt. While any game pays on putts, `flags.missingPutts` names those holes, a warning names the player, his days and the games that wait, Comité › Tarjetas lists them under «Pendientes» with «Ver» (the card marks each hole «sin putts»), and «Cerrar torneo» blocks until they are captured.

### 5.8 Prize pool check

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Individual Stableford, places 1–4 | $20,000 |
| Los Matrimonios | $3,000 |
| Mejor ronda (2 days) | $2,400 |
| La Víbora | $3,600 |
| Menos putts | $1,000 |
| **Total** | **$30,000 = 12 × $2,500** |

The wizard and Comité › Torneo refuse settings that don't balance, and the engine checks the sum against the real field on every compute (`checkPrizePool` in `computeTournament`, MONEY-06): past setup an imbalance is `flags.poolWarning`, on En vivo and Dinero, never a throw that stops the boards (ARCH-19, 2026-10-16: the throwing `assertPrizePool` only tests called is gone). Settings can't check it when they load (`readSettings`, ARCH-04): the sum needs the field.

Balanced is not enough (MONEY-09, 2026-10-07): every place with a prize needs someone who can take it. Individual places can't outnumber the format's entrants (players, or teams under a team format or fourball: drawn ones, else half the field). Pair places can't outnumber the pairs, and a low score's places can't outnumber its entrants. Otherwise the wizard and the Comité refuse to create or save (`checkPrizePool().ok`). Once play starts, a place the field can no longer fill is a warning. An entrant with no result (never played, or a fourball side with no match) takes no paid place. Once final, each game names the places nobody filled and their pesos, which stay with the bank until the Comité decides.

Parsing is not sense either (MONEY-13, 2026-10-13; `src/engine/settings/sense.ts`). The schema accepted a handicap cap of 0 (everyone scratch, silently), a Calcutta paying the same place twice or a place no entrant can take (counted as the main standings count them: teams under a team format), a buyback past 50 % (§5.9), a house cut bigger than the entries, a day's cut past 18 strokes, a currency other than MXN (the only one any screen formats) and a time zone that does not exist. `settingsSense` names each one, and the wizard's «Crear» and Comité › Torneo's «Guardar» refuse while any remains (Torneo lists them by Guardar, on what is stored as well as on a draft, and the money bar stops reading «Cuadra»). They are not in the schema on purpose: a stored tournament whose settings fail to parse can't be read (ARCH-04, below), so a stricter schema would stop a real tournament's boards the day it shipped. A 0 % allowance is a scratch event on purpose, said beside the field. «Pueden pujar invitados» was wired to nothing (§18.4: guests don't bid) and is no longer offered; the key stays so stored settings parse.

Reading settings across builds (ARCH-04, 2026-10-15; `src/engine/settings/read.ts`). Phones run different builds during an event (an update waits for its «Actualizar»), so a row may hold what a newer build wrote. One side game of a type the build didn't know used to fail the whole parse, and the store then computed the tournament on `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` (100 % handicap, no side games, $0 in prizes) without a word; a key it didn't know was dropped, and a Comité save in Torneo wrote every key back from the defaults. Now `readSettings`:
- Sets aside a side game whose type this build doesn't know. The rest computes; `flags.missingGames` names it and Juegos lists it as unknown. If it may move money (anything but `money.source: 'none'`), `flags.moneyUnknown` takes every peso off the screens (`useShowMoney`, as on a «Sin dinero» link), Dinero and the Ceremonia wait, and En vivo says why.
- Refuses a row holding a key outside the schema's tree (`src/engine/settings/settingsSpec.json`, the link's own list, held to the schema) or a value the schema refuses. `settingsError` says what it didn't recognize. Every tournament screen but Más, the TV, the Ceremonia, the printable cards and the Comité's sections but Datos and Historial show «Actualiza la app» with the update bar's button (`SettingsGuard`); the Comité also sees what it didn't recognize.
- `settingsWrite` refuses (`SettingsOutdated`) a save over a row this build can't read, and a save of `games` while the row holds a game it doesn't know, which `games` written back would drop.
- On a link to watch, the page's own pick (`spectatorSettings`) would have dropped what it doesn't know before the store looked: a newer build's key now gives the page no settings at all (it says to update), and a side game it doesn't know goes on with its id, type, name and money source only (none «Sin dinero»; a side pot left out).
Both production rows, every fixture and the seeds the app reads read whole when it shipped. The server still stores what it is sent: every write comes from a build's parsed settings, key by key (`update_settings`). Tests: `src/engine/settings/read.test.ts`, `src/screens/tournament/SettingsGuard.test.tsx`, `src/data/settingsWrite.test.ts`.

### 5.9 La Calcutta (separate pot)
- **When:** the dinner the night before Day 1.
- **Lots:** every player is auctioned once, in an order drawn from a hat.
- **Bidding:**
  - Each player opens his own lot at **$250** and is the default high bidder.
  - Bids go up in **$250** increments.
  - If nobody tops the opening bid, the player owns himself.
- **Limits:** maximum **3 players per owner**. Bidders are the 12 players; a setting can allow guests.
- **Buyback:** right after the hammer, the player may buy back **up to 50%** of himself by paying his owner the same proportion of the price (50% costs half the price). This is paid directly, player to owner.
- **Pot:** the Calcutta pot is the sum of all hammer prices. It's paid to the banker and 100% of it is paid out:

| Slot | Share of the Calcutta pot |
|---|---|
| Stableford champion | 55% |
| Runner-up | 20% |
| Best finisher from tier C | 10% |
| Best finisher from tier D | 10% |
| Last place (La Cuchara de Palo) | 5% |

- **Payout rules:**
  - Each player cashes **at most one slot: the highest** he qualifies for. If a C or D player finishes 1st or 2nd, his tier slot passes to the next-best finisher from that tier.
  - Placings come from the final individual Stableford ranking, including countback.
  - Ties at a slot boundary: the tied players split the combined slots evenly.
  - Each slot's money is split among the player's owners by ownership percentage.
  - Round to whole pesos, and give any rounding remainder to the champion's owners so the payout totals the pot exactly. A slot nobody can fill stays unassigned in whole pesos (floor), so payouts plus unassigned equal the pot to the peso (MONEY-08).
  - The remainder goes to the champion's first owner (his buyer when he bought part of himself back). With no champion's slot paid (a custom payout of tier slots only, or the champion cashing a bigger tier slot), it goes to the first owner of the highest slot paid, never to the player himself for holding the lot (MONEY-17).
  - Every screen shows a slot as the whole pesos it hands over (`SlotResult.pesos`: its owners' shares, each floored, plus the rounding when its owner takes it; an unfilled slot's floored amount), never pot × share worked out again. Its «¿Cómo se calculó?» goes from the share to those pesos («5% de $11,750 = $587.50», «En pesos enteros: $587»). The TV's «Lo que está en juego» shows `AuctionState.stakes` (the payout on the pot as it stands, in the settings' order, the rounding pesos to the champion's slot). Both add up to the pot exactly (MONEY-16).
  - A lot never auctioned (still pending or open once the tournament is live) cashes nothing: the slots' places count among the sold lots, and the Comité sees a warning naming the player (MONEY-11, 2026-10-07; the other reading, self-owned at the opening bid, is Diego's call in the handoff).
  - A sold player with no result (he never played a hole) cashes nothing either, as the main event gives him no place (MONEY-09): the slots go to the finishers with a result, so La Cuchara is the last of them, and with nobody's result (every day rained out) no slot is filled and the whole pot is «Por asignar», whose refund gives each buyer back what he paid (2026-10-09, round 3 of PR 104).
- **Payment deadline:** everything is paid before bed on Calcutta night ("se paga antes de dormir").

## 6. Scoring engine (`src/engine/`)

Pure functions, no I/O, no React. The entry point is `computeTournament(snapshot, settings): TournamentState`.

`TournamentState` includes:
- **Per player, per day, per hole:** strokes received, net score, points, putts, pick-up flag.
- **Per player:** totals and "thru" (holes completed).
- **Handicaps:** the Day 2 cut and PH2 per player.
- **Standings:**
  - Individual, with countback and position labels like `T3`.
  - Best round per day.
  - Matrimonios.
  - Fewest putts.
- **La Víbora, per group per day:**
  - The holder history (hole by hole).
  - The current holder.
  - Pending tiebreak questions.
  - Payouts once the group finishes.
- **Calcutta:**
  - The pot.
  - Who fills each slot.
  - Payouts per owner, both "si terminara ahora" and final.
- **Money:**
  - Prizes per person by category, both live and final.
  - Net per person.
  - Settlement transfers (section 11).
- **Stats and awards** (section 12).
- **Derived feed events:** birdies, lead changes, snake passes, and Nacho's holes.
- **Status flags:** incomplete rounds, pending snake tiebreaks, unsigned cards, score discrepancies.

Every computed number carries an explanation object so the UI can render "¿Cómo se calculó?". For example: base 20 → 80% = 16 → 1 stroke on SI 1–16 → 5 on a par 4 = net par = 2 points. Each one leads with a sentence in words, then its arithmetic (2026-10-16, COPY-19): «5 golpes en un par 4, con 1 golpe de ventaja en este hoyo (índice de golpe 3): 4 neto, par neto, 2 pts», «Un par neto vale 2 pts y cada golpe de diferencia suma o resta uno: 4 + 1 − 5 + 2 = 2 pts», «Se juega con el 80%: 80% de 20 = 16», «El día 1 hizo 37 pts: 37 − 36 = 1 pt de más; se recorta 1 golpe por cada 2 puntos de más: …», and a prize «Camilo va 1.º con 60 pts, si el torneo terminara ahora», «El 1.º lugar paga $10,000». Only the words changed: every number test holds (`src/engine/core/explanations.test.ts`). Since ARCH-05 (2026-10-16) the engine returns each line as a code and its figures, and `src/i18n/explain.ts` writes it in the tournament's currency («¿Cómo se calculó?» and the screen write money the same way); the words above are `explain`'s in es-MX.ts (§4, «The engine says codes»).

```
any hole picked up → 0 pts
```

**Day 2 cut**
```
P1: 35 → 0 · 36 → 0 · 37 → 0 · 38 → 1 · 39 → 1 · 40 → 2 · 42 → 3 · 44 → 4 · 47 → 4 (max)
PH1 16, P1 42 → PH2 13
PH1 2,  P1 44 → PH2 0 (floor at 0)
```

**Countback**
```
A and B both 70 total; Day 2: A 36, B 34 → A ahead
Equal Day 2; Day 2 holes 10–18: A 18, B 17 → A ahead
Equal all the way through hole 18 → tied; they split the prizes for the places they occupy
Two players tied for 1st after countback → each gets ($10,000 + $5,000) / 2 = $7,500
```

**La Víbora**
```
Group [A, B, C, D], starting hole 1:
  hole 3:  B takes 3 putts           → holder B
  hole 7:  C and D both take 3 putts, answer "D holed out last" → holder D
  hole 15: A takes 4 putts           → holder A
  end → B, C, D get $200 each; A gets $0
Nobody takes 3 putts all round → A, B, C, D get $150 each
Starting hole 10: play order is 10–18 then 1–9, so a 3-putt on hole 2 comes after one on hole 18
Two 3-putts on the same hole with no tiebreak answer → state "pendiente", no payout until answered
```

**Calcutta**
```
Pot $12,000 → slots $6,600 / $2,400 / $1,200 / $1,200 / $600
Champion is a C player; the 3rd-place player is also C → Best C slot goes to the 3rd-place player
Runner-up is a D player → Best D slot goes to the next-best D player
Champion owned 50% by owner X and 50% by himself after buyback → X $3,300, champion $3,300
Two players tied for 1st after full countback → each gets (55% + 20%) / 2 = 37.5% of the pot
Rounding remainder goes to the champion's owners; payouts sum exactly to the pot
```

**Money**
```
Prize settings must sum to $30,000
The settlement nets to zero across all people (banker included)
```

## 7. Data model, auth, and permissions

### Tables (Postgres, snake_case)

**Organizers and tournaments**
- `organizers`: `auth_user_id` (PK), `display_name`, `created_at`. Anyone with an email account.
- `tournaments`: `id`, `slug`, `name`, `tagline`, `logo_url`, `accent_color`, `join_code` (6 chars, unique), `status` (`setup` | `auction` | `live` | `finished`), `current_round_id`, `settings` (jsonb, section 18: enabled modules, their parameters, labels, entry fee, handicap rules, tiers), `banker_player_id`, `timezone`, `currency`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `handicaps_locked_at` (0046: when the base handicaps were fixed; see the handicap lock below).
- **A tournament's zone and currency are its settings** (2026-10-16, migration 0074, ARCH-06). `settings.timezone` and `settings.currency` are the source: the wizard, Comité › Torneo and `update_settings` write them. The columns `timezone` and `currency` were a second copy nothing wrote (every tournament read Mazatlán and MXN there, whatever the organizer chose); they are now the settings' mirror for SQL readers (the Admin de Polo, a report, an older bundle), written by the trigger `tournaments_settings_mirror` on every insert and update (a write of the column alone changes nothing; settings with no usable value keep what the column had), and 0074 brought every stored row to its settings with the history's trigger off. The app never reads the columns (`TournamentRow` has no such fields; Historial leaves them out as bookkeeping). Every money render takes the tournament's currency: `formatMoney(amount, currency)` has no default, `<Money>` and `useCurrency()` (`src/components/currency.ts`) read it from `TournamentCurrency`, which the gates (TournamentGate, SpectatorGate, FixtureGate) put around every screen of a tournament; a screen outside one passes what its data carries (a Ronda rápida, the platform's `PLATFORM_CURRENCY`). Moments of a tournament (Historial, the player sheet, the handicap changes, a share card's date, a restore's file) are in `settings.timezone`. MXN stays the only currency the settings allow (`CURRENCIES` in sense.ts): the engine's «¿Cómo se calculó?» writes amounts as pesos (`pesos()`), and a second currency needs them to take it first. Tests: `supabase/tests/settings_mirror.sql`, `upgrade_0074.sh`, `src/screens/tournament/currency.test.tsx` (a stored row in USD: Dinero, Juegos and En vivo in dollars). **Release order: either way** (the bundle never reads the columns).
- `update_settings(p_tournament_id, p_seen, p_set)` (2026-10-13, migration 0043, ARCH-13): the Comité's setup, key by key. Torneo and Juegos wrote the whole settings blob, so whichever saved last undid the other (a Torneo draft opened before a game changed in Juegos, or on the other phone, wrote the old games back) and nobody was told. `p_seen` is what the screen started from, `p_set` only what it changed, each an object of `settings` (top-level keys; a key the row lacks is seen as null, a key set to null is removed) and the brand columns `name` (trimmed, never blank), `tagline` (blank is null), `accent_color`; every key set must be seen (else 22023, «No pudimos leer ese cambio…»). Under the tournament's row lock each seen key is compared with the row (jsonb: by content, key order aside): one that moved refuses the whole save, `{ok: false, changed: ['settings.games', 'name'], now}`, and nothing is written; else only the set keys are, `{ok: true, now}` (nothing set, nothing written). Security invoker after its own Comité check (42501): the update meets `tournaments_update` and the column grants as a direct write does. Torneo sees every key (its prize check read them all, so what is stored is what it checked) and writes the ones it changed; refused, it lays its draft over the row (`mergeOver`: what only it changed stays, the rest is theirs, a key both changed is theirs and the toast says so) and the next Guardar sends it. Juegos sees and writes only `games`, so a Torneo save of anything else in between stays, and a games change in between refuses Juegos' and reads the games again. An untouched accent is never written. `src/data/settingsWrite.ts`, `api.updateSettings` (a refusal is `SettingsChanged`); direct writes of `settings` stay for older bundles until the contract (PLAN §2.7). Tests: `supabase/tests/update_settings.sql`, `race_update_settings.sh` (two saves at once, and an old build's whole-blob update open), the `update_settings:` cases, `src/screens/admin/settingsSaves.test.tsx`. **Release order: 0043 reaches production before the bundle** (Torneo and Juegos save only through it).
- `tournament_organizers`: `tournament_id`, `auth_user_id`, `role` (`owner` | `admin`).
- `courses`: `id`, `name`, `created_by`. Reusable across tournaments.
- `tees`: `id`, `course_id`, `name`, `color`, `rating`, `slope`, `par_total`, `gender` (nullable).
- `courses` also carry `source` (`manual` | `golfcourseapi` | `scorecard_photo`), `external_id`, `imported_at`, `location`.
- `holes`: `tee_id`, `number` (1–18), `par`, `stroke_index`, optional `yards`. Unique on (`tee_id`, `number`).
- `save_course(p)` (2026-10-13, migration 0045, ARCH-13) writes a course's card in one call: the course, every tee in the order given with its holes (par total summed), and the tees taken out, or none of it. It was 3 + 2 per tee + 2 requests, so a failure halfway left some tees saved and others not on a course every tournament that plays it shares. Security invoker after its own checks (`can_edit_course`, or `can_manage_courses` for a new course; only tees of that course, 22023 otherwise), so every write meets the policies a direct write meets. What no caller's policies can see, every use of a tee in other tournaments, the triggers on `tees` see on every path (save_course, an older bundle's direct delete, a course's cascade): 0010's `tees_guard` refuses deleting a tee a round plays, and 0045's `tees_guard_default` one a player has as default, each refusing the whole card. Before, the phone checked the round tees under its own policies, which see only its tournaments: a tee another tournament's round played reached the delete, which `tees_guard` refused after the rest of the card was written, and a tee that was a player's default in any tournament was deleted and the default nulled without a word.
- **Verified cards and the card's history** (2026-10-16, migration 0075, STRAT-14; plan in `docs/strategy/course-catalog.md`). `courses.card_changed_at` is the last change of the card (its tees and holes) after the course was first saved, a tee or hole added included; `card_corrected_at`/`card_corrected_by` the last change or removal of what it held (a hole's par, stroke index or yards; a tee's name, color, rating, slope, par or gender; a tee or hole taken out), so a card that arrived after its course (a location-only hit, then the photo) is changed, never corrected. The transaction that created the course (its `created_at` is `now()` all through it) is no change, nor a save that writes the same values (save_course upserts every hole). `verified_at`, `verified_by`, `verified_via` (`platform` | `comite`): a person compared the card with the printed one; any change of the card takes it off (triggers `tees_card_changed`, `holes_card_changed`, through `course_card_mark`). Nobody writes those six columns directly, any role (`courses_card_guard`, 42501); only the functions, under `cardi.course_card`. `platform_verify_course(course, on, reason)`: the Admin de Polo, on or off, a reason of 3–200 (`trim_reason`), logged (`course_verify`, `course_unverify`); never a card with no tee or a tee with `tee_problems`. `verify_course(course, tournament, reason)`: the Comité of that tournament, on only, and only when all hold: the tournament is Terminado, counts for stats and is no Ronda rápida; one of its days on the course is finished with every hole scored for someone; the card has not changed in any way since its first hole there (the audit log's first score line, or a score's own stamp if earlier); every tee is sound. The reason lands on the course's history line (`cardi.reason`); a verified card answers as it is. Unverified cards stay usable. «Tarjeta verificada» (`VerifiedBadge`) shows on Comité › Campos' list, in a search's «Ya en Polo» (the catalog's own matches, before the providers', so a course someone loaded is used, not imported again), in the editor (which says a change takes it off) and in the Admin de Polo's Campos; inside a Terminado tournament the Comité has «Verificar tarjeta» on the courses its days played. `listCourses` reads `*` so a database without 0075 lists the catalog as before. A photo that fills a location-only course makes its source `scorecard_photo`: the card came from the photo. Tests: `supabase/tests/course_catalog.sql`, `src/screens/admin/AdminCourses.verified.test.tsx`, `src/data/api.courses.test.ts`. **Release order: 0075 reaches production before the bundle** (`verify_course`, the Admin's filters).

**People and teams**

- **The handicap lock** (2026-10-13, migration 0046, UX-28; production first, then the bundle). The brief fixes the base handicaps before the Calcutta (§5.2) and the runbook said «bloqueado», but the player sheet changed one at any moment with no reason and only the audit log saw it. Fixed means `tournaments.handicaps_locked_at` is set («Fijar hándicaps», `lock_handicaps(tid)`: the Comité, idempotent; the tournament leaving setup sets it by itself, trigger `tournaments_handicap_lock`) or the tournament is not in setup (one under way before 0046 has no moment and is fixed all the same; `handicapsLocked` in `src/engine/core/handicapLock.ts`). Only those two and a restore write the column (a direct write is 42501). While fixed, the trigger `players_handicap_lock` refuses a change to what a handicap is computed from (`handicap_used(source, base, index, estimate)`: an estimate's three scores, an index or the base when none was typed, else the base; a base left over under an index is no change; the engine's `baseHandicap` reads it the same way) unless the statement carries the Comité's reason: `update_player(p_player_id, p_row, p_reason)` (invoker; the Comité; the sheet's own fields in one statement, never the tournament, the account link or the note; reason 3–200 after `trim_reason`, 0028's whitespace set). The refusal (22023, «Los hándicaps ya están fijados: para cambiar uno hace falta un motivo») meets every path, an older bundle's direct write included. Each change lands on `players.handicap_note` (`{from: the handicap when fixed, changes: [{at, reason, to}]}`, the 20 latest; written only by the trigger, cleared by «Liberar», null on a new player) and, through `audit_row` (which now reads a statement's `cardi.reason` when the row has no reason column), in Historial. Everyone reads it: `state.flags.handicapChanges` (a change undone shows nothing) is a caution line on En vivo that opens the player, and his sheet lists each change with its moment and reason (`HandicapChanges`). «Liberar» (`unlock_handicaps(tid, reason)`) only while the tournament is in setup: the lock and the notes go. «Para empezar» has the line «Revisa los hándicaps y fíjalos»; opening the auction says it fixes them. `restore_tournament` (0030's body plus both columns, the players written under `cardi.restore`) brings back the backup's lock and notes whatever the lock says now. The tee is not part of the lock. Tests: `supabase/tests/handicap_lock.sql`, `restore_roundtrip.sql`, `src/engine/core/handicapLock.test.ts`, `src/screens/admin/AdminPlayers.lock.test.tsx`, `src/screens/tournament/LiveScreen.handicaps.test.tsx`.
- `device_sessions`: `auth_user_id` (PK), `player_id`, `created_at`. Maps anonymous auth users to players.
- `pin_claims` (2026-10-13, migration 0050, TRUST-24): each time a phone took a player with his PIN. `id`, `tournament_id`, `player_id`, `auth_user_id`, `platform` (`ios` | `android` | `desktop` | `other`, read from the request's user agent by `request_platform()`; nothing else of the phone is kept), `claimed_at`, `released_at`, `released_by` (the Comité's account when it took the phone off). One open claim per player and phone (the same phone typing the PIN again keeps its first). `claim_player` ends the phone's claim of another player and starts this one; `release_device` («Cambiar de jugador», «No soy yo») ends its claim. RLS on, no policies, no client grants; audited, so each claim, letting go and taking off is a line of Comité › Historial; in the nightly backup, not in a tournament's export. Before, any phone that typed a PIN became the player without a word, and the history showed its writes as his. `tournament_audit` now adds `phone` (`{n, of, kind}`: its number among the phones that ever held the player, in the order they took him) to each change a player made from one of them, once he has had more than one (never for a change from his own account), and leaves account ids out of the images (`auth_user_id`, `released_by`; since 0063 also `created_by`, `voided_by`, `profile_id` and an organizer row's `rowId`). `player_phones(tid)` (the Comité: each open claim's id, player, kind, since, n of N; never an account id) and `release_pin_claim(id, reason)` (the Comité; reason 3–200 after `trim_reason`; the claim ends with the reason in the history, and that phone's `device_sessions` row goes, so it has to type the PIN again and what it had waiting waits for that PIN; since 0052 any push address it saved for him goes with it, as on every way a claim ends). On an iPhone, Safari and the app on the home screen are two anonymous users, so two phones are often one person: the copy numbers the phones («teléfono 2 de 2, iPhone») and never says someone else wrote. The claims standing when 0050 ran were backfilled from `device_sessions`, their kind from the phone's last heartbeat. Tests: `supabase/tests/pin_phones.sql` (the finding's scenario: two phones holding Ana, each writing a hole, told apart), `src/screens/admin/AdminHistory.phones.test.tsx`, `AdminPlayers.phones.test.tsx`. **Release order: 0050 reaches production before the bundle** (the nightly backup copies `pin_claims`). Left: the player himself isn't told when another phone takes his PIN (a PIN player has no inbox), and the PIN is still the Comité's, not one the player picks.
- **What the Comité typed about you** (2026-10-16, migration 0070, TRUST-16). Most people Polo keeps data on never signed up. After the PIN (`me.via === 'device'`), En vivo shows once per tournament and player on that phone «Esto es lo que el Comité guardó de ti» (`MyDataCard`: name, photo or not, tier, handicap and what it comes from, form guide, and that the whole tournament sees it but the PIN), with «Pedir corrección»: `request_player_correction(tid, message)` (his own player only, 3–500 after `trim_reason`, five open at most) into `player_corrections` (RLS on, no policies, no grants; audited; in the nightly backup, not in a tournament's export), which the Comité lists in Comité › Jugadores (`player_corrections(tid)`, `CorrectionsCard`) and marks done (`resolve_player_correction`). **Tenant key** (2026-10-10, migration 0030, DB-12; on production since 2026-10-10)
- `tournament_id` (NOT NULL) is on every round-, group- and lot-scoped table: `groups`, `group_members`, `round_tees`, `scores`, `snake_tiebreaks`, `card_signatures`, `handicap_overrides`, `hole_awards`, `photos`, `calcutta_bids`, `calcutta_buybacks` (`team_members`, `rejected_writes` and `money_adjustments` had it already). A composite key `(tournament_id, <parent>) → parent (tournament_id, id)`, on update and delete cascade, means a child can never name another tournament than its round's, group's or lot's (`rounds_tenant_uk`, `groups_tenant_uk`, `calcutta_lots_tenant_uk`); each has an index `(tournament_id, <parent>)`.
- A BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE trigger (`<table>_tenant`, `tenant_from_round/group/lot`, definer) fills it from the parent when the writer leaves it out, which every bundle does, and again when an update moves the row to another parent without naming it. An upsert's update path keeps the row's own. A phone cannot write `scores.tournament_id` (no column grant); on the other tables a writer may name it, and the key refuses one that disagrees (23503).
- `restore_tournament` refuses a backup whose child rows name another tournament (22023) and writes every child row with its own; a backup from before 0030 has no tenant on them and restores. `duplicate_tournament` copies no child table.
- **A row names only its tournament's players** (2026-10-15, migration 0057, SEC-11). Every key from a tournament's table to `players` (a score and its writer, group members, tees of the day, overrides, tiebreaks, claims, signers, lots and their owners, bids, payments, pairs, game entries and results, money assignments, rejected writes, PIN claims and push addresses, notices, results, telemetry, and `tournaments.banker_player_id`) carries the row's tournament with the player, against `players (id, tournament_id)` (or `(tournament_id, id)` where an index already leads with the tournament), under its old name and with its old action on delete (cascade, or that column alone set to null). Before, a writer allowed on its own tournament's rows (the Comité, a definer) could name another tournament's player and the database took it; only `card_signatures` and `team_members` were bound. One key per pair of tables still, so no embed changed. A round can no longer move to another tournament while its rows name its players. `supabase/tests/player_tenant_keys.sql` fails on any later table whose key to `players` leaves the tournament out. Production had no row naming another tournament's player when it ran.
- **Two keys to each parent, so an embed names one.** Each child reaches its parent by the old key and the composite one, and PostgREST refuses an embed between them that doesn't say which (PGRST201, the whole request fails): write `group_members!group_members_group_id_fkey(player_id)`, never `group_members(player_id)`. `src/lib/embedHints.test.ts` reads every request in `src`, `scripts`, `api` and `e2e` and fails on one that doesn't (PR 111's verifier found two, in `scripts/simulate.mjs` and `scripts/rls-test.mjs`).
- **The audit log names the tournament from the row itself.** A child row deleted by a cascade (a round, group or lot deleted) used to be logged with no tournament; since 0030 it is logged under its tournament and shows in Comité › Historial (paged by id, 200 at most, so a large cascade is many pages, never a broken one).
- The backfill in 0030 ran with each table's user triggers off and put each back in its own mode, so it wrote no audit row, recomputed no result and moved no `version` or `updated_at` (`supabase/tests/upgrade_0030.sh`). `supabase/tests/harness/revert_0030.sql` (plus 0029's `restore_tournament`) is the way back, proven to give 0029's policies and rows exactly.

**Rounds and scoring**
- `rounds`: `id`, `tournament_id`, `number` (1..N), `date`, `course_id`, `holes` (9 | 18), `status` (`scheduled` | `live` | `finished` | `cancelled`).
- `groups`: `id`, `round_id`, `number`, `tee_time`, `start_hole` (1 | 10).
- `group_members`: `group_id`, `player_id`.
- `round_tees`: `round_id`, `player_id`, `tee_id`. Unique on (`round_id`, `player_id`). Which tee each player plays that day (§13b-D). (When the pairs module is on, a group is normally two pairs; the engine validates that, the schema doesn't require it.)
- `scores`: `id`, `round_id`, `player_id`, `hole`, `strokes` (nullable), `putts` (nullable), `picked_up`, `entered_by`, `client_ts`, `updated_at`, `version` (bumped on every change), `device_id`, `mutation_id`. Unique on (`round_id`, `player_id`, `hole`). A direct write from the app records the session's own player as `entered_by`, whatever the body says, and no device or mutation (those are `save_hole`'s) (`scores_00_writer`, SEC-02). The account that wrote is in `audit_log.actor_auth_user_id`, not on the row: every member reads the scores.
- `save_hole(p)` (2026-10-07, migration 0026, on production since 2026-10-08; the Tarjeta saves through it since outbox v2, REL-05): a group's hole in one statement, at most 8 entries and 16 KB (more is refused whole, 22023). Each entry names the fields the phone set and, as `base`, the whole row it saw (`{}` or null when it saw none; no `base` key at all writes blind, as a direct write does, so a client must never drop it); a field someone else changed meanwhile isn't overwritten, and the answer says `conflict` with the server's row. A pick-up also writes «no strokes» and a number of strokes «not picked up», both checked against what the phone saw (a key its base leaves out reads as no strokes, no putts, not picked up), so a stale phone's strokes never vanish under another's pick-up, nor the other way round. Entries the server refuses (round not live, card signed, not in the group, invalid) answer `rejected` and, with conflicts, go to `rejected_writes`, one row per player per call. A mutation sent twice answers what it answered the first time (`score_mutations`: account, tournament, round, hole, answer; no client access); the same id from another account or for another hole is refused (22023). It locks the hole (`save_hole:<round>:<hole>`, which `admin_save_score` and `resolve_score_dispute` take too) and the hole's rows at once, in player order. Only a player of the group calls it: the Comité outside the group (an organizer with no player, an admin player of another group) still writes directly while the day is live, and corrects a closed day or a signed card through `admin_save_score`. Direct writes stay allowed for older bundles until the contract (PLAN §5.1). The in-memory server (`src/data/testing/fakeSupabase.ts`) has the same rules, pinned by the `save_hole:` cases of `cases/serverRules.json`.
- `rejected_writes`: `tournament_id`, `round_id`, `hole`, `player_id`, `writer_player_id`, `auth_user_id`, `device_id`, `mutation_id`, `payload`, `reason` (`round_not_live` | `card_signed` | `not_in_group` | `invalid` | `conflict`), `status` (`open` | `applied` | `dismissed`), `resolved_by`, `resolved_at`, `resolution_note` (all 0026). Inserted only by `save_hole` (the entry as the phone sent it: since 0028's bundle an untouched default carries `auto: true`, unless the player confirmed it with «Sí, todos par»), changed only by `resolve_rejected_write`; the Comité reads its tournament's, a phone its own (REL-08). A conflict is the phone's, never the Comité's (it sends its value again over an untouched default, or asks the player «Dejar el suyo» / «Guardar el mío» in every state of its Tarjeta, any day or group, a closed day included, until someone answers on that phone; «Guardar el mío» on a closed day is refused as a typed value and so reaches the Comité): its row stays `open` as the record and is never listed, warned about or waited on. A question nobody answers stays only on that phone, and the card keeps the first value. An `auto` entry is listed only when nothing else covers its hole (no score for that player and hole, no typed refusal of it open); over a real score it is nobody's capture. Not in a tournament's backup (the server's record of what it refused, not a fact of the tournament; the nightly backup has it), so `restore_tournament` leaves it. Published to Realtime by 0028 and on the live channel since 0028 reached production (2026-10-10, `INBOX_TABLES`): a change never touches the boards; it reads the Comité's «Pendientes de revisar» again, coalesced (`inboxChanged`, 300 ms), on a phone whose list is loaded for that tournament (§8). The Comité also reads it when the screen opens and after each answer.

- **Player accounts** (2026-09-28, `src/data/account.ts`, `src/screens/profile/`). `/entrar` asks for an email and a 6-digit code. An anonymous device converts in place (`updateUser({ email })` + the `email_change` code: same uid, so its PIN claim and outbox stay valid); an address that already has an account signs in with a code, and the device's player travels in a link token (stashed in `sessionStorage` with the player's names). After any sign-in `finishProfileSignIn()` creates the profile (named after the player it came from, migration 0014) and saves the device's player in it. Switching account (`refuseWithUnsent` before every way: the organizer's email sign-in at `/organizer/login`, `/entrar`'s code when there is no session to convert and again when the code is typed, Google's sign-in as well as its link) or signing out is refused while the outbox holds unsent writes for any tournament (`unsentWrites()`; writes a newer build queued don't count, they stay on the phone for it), and the refusal names the tournament (from the name each write keeps, once the saved boards are gone) and says whether signal or the PIN sends them. Converting this anonymous device in place is no switch (same uid) and goes ahead with writes queued. A device holds one PIN claim (`claim_player` replaces it), so Entrar refuses a PIN while another tournament still has writes that go out with this phone (`refuseClaimWithUnsent`, outbox `unsentBeforeClaim`: not the tournament being entered, not writes held for a PIN elsewhere); and the gate's `leave()` («Cambiar de jugador», «No soy yo») releases the claim only when it is this tournament's (`myDeviceClaim()`, unless the membership came from it), never another tournament's. When a tournament's own slug leads nowhere (deleted), its queued writes move to the rejected list («El torneo ya no existe»), so they no longer hold the phone. Likewise a hole of a round the Comité deleted (`rejectGoneRounds`, run by the PIN's adopt and by the gate once the boards come from the server): `save_hole` answers `not_member` for a round that does not exist, which held the hole for a PIN that could never send it. When the server's boards lack the hole's round and the hole was queued before that fetch set out, the server is asked again with the session, in one request (the tournament with its rounds embedded, `rounds!rounds_tournament_id_fkey`, so membership is read once); only a round absent while the tournament row reads moves the hole to the rejected list («Ese día del torneo ya no existe»). Any doubt (an error, no row, no session, no signal), a hole on its way to the server, or one changed meanwhile rejects nothing. Signing out waits past auth-js's own logout deadline (`SIGN_OUT_TIMEOUT_MS`, 15 s, «Cerrando sesión…»: on lie-fi a valid session ends at 12 s) and clears the profile, «Tu último torneo» and the boards saved on the phone only once the session is really gone (`signOut()` returns that; with no signal and an expired token, or inside auth-js's 60 s retry cooldown, auth-js keeps it and the app says the session could not be confirmed). If auth-js ends it after the wait (an expired token still refreshing), the same cleanup runs then and the shell goes home and says so (`LateSignOutHome`). Signing out ends every session of the account (`signOut({ scope: 'global' })`, SEC-07), so a phone lost or lent is signed out from any other, and Editar perfil says so under the button. A PIN the server takes forgets the other tournaments this phone held only by its PIN, their boards and who it was there (`clearPinEntriesExcept`, TRUST-15): the phone's one claim moved, and with no signal it used to open one of them as the player it had been. A sign-out the person asked for is not a lost session to the tournament gate (`signedOutOnPurpose()`) until a real sign-in (`SIGNED_IN`, never a token refresh, and not of the account auth-js is still signing out), and a gate that was open when it was asked for stops its retries (`signOutsAsked()`), so no anonymous user starts behind it. Accounts get **Mi Polo** as home (`/`); with the last tournament's boards saved on the phone, «Tu último torneo» shows there while the profile loads and keeps its place once it lands if it was on screen first or the account's list lacks it; profiles live at `/p/<handle>`, the editor at `/perfil/editar`. `e2e/profile.mjs` runs both paths with real emails (a throwaway mail.tm inbox).

### Permissions (RLS)
- **Tenant boundary is the tournament.** Every policy starts from `tournament_id`: a device linked to one tournament reads nothing from another. `my_player_id(tournament_id)` and `is_tournament_organizer(tournament_id)` are the two helpers.
- **Reads are set-based** (0030, DB-12, SEC-05). `my_tournament_ids()` (STABLE, definer, signed-in roles only) returns the caller's tournaments, the same set `is_tournament_member` answers true for: organizer rows, confirmed profile links, this device's PIN claim; the platform admin gets every tournament. Every tournament table's read policy is `tournament_id = any ((select public.my_tournament_ids())::uuid[])` (`tournaments`: `id`), `to authenticated`: one call per statement, then an array test per row. A 60-player reload went from about 2.2 s to about 11 ms of database time on the harness (`supabase/tests/harness/perf/`). Write policies keep their exact expressions; every FOR ALL policy is split into INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE (so none is OR'd into SELECT), and so are `tees` and `holes`, whose reads stay `auth.uid() is not null`. A new tournament table gets the same read policy and per-command writes, never FOR ALL. `supabase/tests/tenant_write_matrix.sql` holds 6,120 reads and writes by ten identities in three round states to the answers main gave before 0030 (`harness/tenant_matrix_expected.sql`); change that file only with a migration meant to change who may do what.
- **Read:** any linked device can read everything in its tournament except `pin_hash`. Hide it with a view or column privileges.
- **Write scores:** allowed when the round is `live`, the card isn't signed yet, and the writer is in the same group for that round or of the Comité (0026: the Comité's direct writes no longer bypass the round and the card, REL-09). ## 8. Realtime and offline

- **Subscriptions:** every table in `src/data/realtimeTables.ts` (only published ones: a channel naming an unpublished table fails whole, REL-01). On any change, update the local snapshot and recompute.
  - **Published on production first.** `ON_PRODUCTION` in `realtimeTables.ts` names the newest migration read back from production (`0049`, 2026-10-13: `select tablename from pg_publication_tables where pubname = 'supabase_realtime'` through the Management API, with each table's `relreplident` and filter column). `realtimeTables.test.ts` refuses a table that only a later migration publishes, so a bundle can never reach phones before its table's publication reaches the database. Move it only after that read, in the change that adds the table, never to make the test pass.
  - **Filtered by tournament** (2026-10-13, migration 0049, DB-05). Each binding carries `filter: tournament_id=eq.<id>` (the tournament row `id=eq.<id>`; `liveFilter`), so the server sends a phone its own tournament's changes and no other's, and checks RLS only for those. Postgres Changes reads a delete's filter from what the delete logged, so every published table but `tournaments` (filtered on its primary key) logs its whole old row (`replica identity full`); a delete's old record still reaches the phone as its primary key only, since RLS is on. `realtimeTables.test.ts` holds the filter column and the identity to the migrations through `ON_PRODUCTION`, `supabase/tests/realtime_filters.sql` holds every published table to both in the catalog, and `e2e/stack/isolation.spec.ts` proves it on a real Realtime: a phone hears its own tournament's insert, update and delete, and none of another's. A table published later needs its `tournament_id` and its full identity before a bundle listens to it. **Release order: 0049 reaches production before the bundle** (a filtered binding on a table without it hears none of its deletes). Bundles from before keep listening unfiltered; the client-side guards below stay, for them and for a server that ignores a filter.
  - The tables that move during play (`APPLIED_TABLES` in `src/data/realtimeApply.ts`: scores, tiebreaks, signatures, contest claims, overrides, tees of the day, payments, the Comité's money assignments (`money_adjustments`: a decision's rows one event each, a void as their update), lots, bids, buybacks, game entries and results) are applied from the event's own row, by key, through the fetch's mappers, at the place a fetch would put it (fetched rows are put in the same order, `inLiveOrder`): a foursome's hole is one recompute and no request (REL-11, PERF-07), and a phone that applied a change and one that fetched it hold the same lists.
  - Another tournament's rows are left alone, at no cost: since 0030 a round's or lot's row carries `tournament_id`, so one naming another tournament is ignored at once instead of waiting with the parked ones. A delete is applied only when its key is one of this snapshot's rows (RLS never checks a delete; the filter keeps other tournaments' away, DB-05); a score's delete names its id, which scores keep. A row of a round or lot not loaded yet waits (`parked`: 2 min, the newest 5,000) and lands with its day or lot, whose own change reloads; a delete forgets what was parked for its row. Payments match by id or by their flow key (kind, from, to), so the row Dinero lays and the server's insert are one.
  - The structural tables (`RELOADED_TABLES`: the tournament row, players, rounds, groups and their members, pairs, teams and their members) reload, coalesced (150 ms), but only for a change that concerns this tournament (`concernsBoards`): an insert or update naming another tournament is left alone (every one carries `tournament_id`; groups and their members since 0030), and a delete, which without the filter reaches every phone listening whatever tournament it was in (DB-05), reloads only when its key is a row the snapshot holds or while a fetch is on its way. A team draw anywhere deletes its members and writes them again, so without this each draw reloaded every phone on the platform. `INBOX_TABLES` (`rejected_writes`) are not part of the snapshot: a change reads the Comité's «Pendientes de revisar» again, coalesced (300 ms), and leaves the boards alone; only a phone whose list is loaded reads it (the Comité's screens fill it), for an insert or update of that tournament or a delete of a row it holds (or while it is being read), so a player's phone, which RLS sends only its own rows, costs nothing, and another tournament's delete reads nothing (DB-05). The phone's own rejected list is the outbox's, not this table's.
  - A fetch (reload, or the gate's `load` of the tournament already open) gets the changes applied since it started (`catchUp`; a delete of a row the boards did not hold is logged only while a fetch is out), so it never takes one back. A visible phone with a live channel and no fetch for five minutes checks it against the server (`HEAL_MS`), never while a fetch is on its way, waiting longer after each failure (10, 20, then 30 min), since a lost change has no sequence to show the gap yet (PLAN §5.2). A fetch that fails on the way (no signal, a timeout, the server down or busy: `isTransient` in `src/lib/humanError.ts`) is tried again after 5 s, doubling to every 2 minutes, while the phone is on screen with signal and the channel is up (when it is down, the 15 s poll is the retry); a refusal or a missing row is not, and waits for the check (REL-10). Until a fetch works the error stays, and the header says «Conectando…» with how old the boards are (`useBoardsMayBeOld`), as the TV's top line does. The gate unsubscribes the store when it goes, and a load still on its way then opens no channel. A channel being left delivers nothing more; leaving clears what it had waiting, a reload it had asked for included.
  - The store keeps the server's rows (`data.base`: what changes apply to and what the phone keeps) apart from what the screens read (`data.snapshot`: `base` plus the outbox overlay). A write the outbox sent lands as the server stored it: each push asks for its rows back (`select()`), and once taken they are applied like a live change, and logged so a fetch on its way replays them, unless the channel already brought their echo (`sameChange`, jsonb in any key order), after which anything newer is in too. A landed score never replaces one the boards hold with a later stamp; the channel's own changes apply in the order they come whatever their stamps, since `updated_at` is when the transaction began and a write that waited on the row's lock commits last with the earlier one. Only what came since the push went out counts as its echo (an identical earlier row is not), echoes parked for a day not loaded included. A fetch that landed while the push was out may have read before the write, after it, or after a later one, and so may an answer older than the change log (2 min): the write lands all the same (a score under its stamp guard), so a hole the phone saved never leaves its boards, and one more fetch says what the server holds, asked once per flush, when it ends; a write that lands while a fetch is out asks for one more after it, and a fetch superseded or failed keeps that ask. These times and the log's age run on `liveClock` (`performance.timeOrigin + performance.now()`), which a clock set back or forward doesn't move. A signature that met the other pair's comes back empty, and a fetch shows theirs. The app's other tabs land them too (BroadcastChannel, with how long ago the push went out, since each tab's clock counts from its own origin, and when the message was posted, so a tab busy when it comes takes the wait off). A tournament left is fetched by nothing. A refused write leaves the boards at once.
  - **The boards keep what did not change** (PERF-09, 2026-10-13; `src/data/share.ts`). Every compute in the store gets the data the screens hold and hands back its own objects wherever the new boards read the same: each row, each table, the tournament row and its parsed settings. When nothing reads differently, it hands back that data itself, so no screen re-renders and the engine doesn't run. The engine is a pure function of the snapshot and the settings (it reads no clock), which is what makes reusing its state safe.
    - Screens read plain values where a whole-board subscription cost a render: the shell its four words, the share button its slug and name, the feed its lines as words (`useBoards`: the last value while the new one reads the same).
    - En vivo's rows are memoized from plain values (`BoardRow`), and its «Actualizado hace…» line is its own component. `useSavedWhen` moves its clock only when the words change.
    - Measured on full12-live (`screenRenders.test.tsx`, with React DevTools' own count):

      - one changed score: the rows it changed plus the board around them, 15 renders (was 414).

The header's age line also counted the holes on the phone, which the Tarjeta's tab badge (REL-17) and its line already say.

A design fixture shows nothing unless it acts a state out (`?net=cache|failed|joining|live` in `FixtureGate`); offline still shows.

- **Writes go through an IndexedDB outbox:**
  - Apply locally first, then push, then retry with backoff.
  - Show a sync chip: "Sincronizado" or "3 pendientes". On the Tarjeta it is the line under «Guardar hoyo», and it says the writes only («3 hoyos por subir», «1 rechazado», the last error); the signal is the header's to say, and with none the last error waits for it (REL-21).
  - **Outbox v2 (REL-05, 2026-10-09):** a group's hole is one `save_hole` call (a `hole` item). Per player only the fields that differ from the row the phone saw when the player was first touched (`base`, `{}` for none, never left out), and only the players touched plus the unplayed ones the save fills with par and 2 putts (`auto`). Each item carries a `mutation_id` made when it was queued and kept across retries and reloads, so a retry after a lost answer gets the first answer back; an item is marked `sent` in IndexedDB before its call goes out, and from then on nothing merges into it (a newer write queues behind it). Unsent items of the same hole merge field by field, the older `base` kept. Answers land entry by entry: stored rows as the server's rows; `rejected` entries go to the rejected list with their reason in Spanish; a 22023 or 42501 refusal rejects the whole call, never retried; `not_member` holds the hole for the PIN (REL-16); the network, a timeout or an unreadable answer retries. Holes queued by older builds (`score` items) still go out as the direct writes they were; an older build reads a `hole` item as a newer build's and leaves it on the phone. Dexie v4 adds the `conflicts` table.
- **Conflicts:** the first committed value wins and the second phone is asked (PLAN Amendment 1, item 12). A `conflict` lands the server's row on the boards and, unless the entry was an untouched default, waits on the Tarjeta (`HoleConflicts`): «Hoyo 7: Diego ya capturó a Justo: 6. No se guardó lo tuyo: 5.», with «Guardar el mío» (a new save whose base is the server's row) and «Dejar el suyo». Every question the phone holds for the tournament shows, in every state of the Tarjeta (another day's names its day: «Día 1, hoyo 7: …»), until it is answered there: the Comité's list never shows a conflict. On a day no longer live «Guardar el mío» is refused as a typed value and reaches «Pendientes de revisar», and the box says so. A value that is exactly what the Tarjeta saves for an untouched player (par for his tee, 2 putts: `dflt`) is no one's claim: the phone's value goes over it at once, so two phones each keeping the other pair (tarjeta cruzada) both land. Two exceptions are claims whatever their value, and the phone is asked: a row with a `reason` (a Comité correction, `admin_save_score`), and a row with `entered_by` null (the Comité's own direct write, by an organizer who plays nobody or the Polo admin). Any value replaced by another phone's is kept as a "discrepancia" (badge, `previous`) until the card is signed. Direct writes (older bundles, the Comité outside the group) are still last write wins, flagged the same way.
- **Offline shell:** the app shell, fonts, logo, and the last snapshot are cached. The app opens and shows the last known boards with no signal.
- **After a deploy** (REL-13): a page no service worker controls (a first visit, WhatsApp's in-app browser) that asks for a screen's chunk the deploy removed loads the new build, once (`vite:preloadError`, `src/lib/chunkReload.ts`): never offline, never twice in a minute (the second failure shows «Algo falló» with «Reiniciar la app»), and the error screen stays blank while it reloads. `vercel.json` leaves `/assets/` out of the SPA rewrite, so a missing chunk is a 404, not the app's HTML. The worker claims a first visit's page as soon as it installs (`clientsClaim`); an update still waits for «Actualizar».
  - **«Actualizar» loads the new build on every page** (QA-16, 2026-10-13; `applyUpdate` in `src/data/appUpdate.ts`). vite-plugin-pwa reloads once the new worker takes the page, but only a page that had a worker when it opened: on a first visit the new worker took the page, nothing reloaded, and the bar stayed with nothing left to apply. The page now reloads itself when its worker changes (`controllerchange`, once), and at once when nothing is waiting any more (another tab took the update). `e2e/fixtures/update.spec.ts` runs the fixtures build's real worker on a server of its own and deploys a new one mid-hole: nothing reloads, the typed value stays, the bar waits for the hole, and «Actualizar» loads the new worker, on a first visit and on a page the worker served from the start (one load, not two).
- **Target:** under 2 seconds from save on one phone to update on the others.

## 11. Money and settlement

**Flows**
- **Entries:** $2,500 × 12 to the banker (the banker is a player set in settings).
- **Calcutta:** hammer prices to the banker.
- **Buybacks:** paid player → owner directly; tracked, with "Pagado" toggles.
- **Payouts:** the banker pays each person his prizes plus his Calcutta shares.

**Per person, show**
- Pagó: entry + Calcutta purchases + buybacks paid.
- Recibe: prizes + Calcutta shares + buybacks received.
- Neto: the difference.

**Checks**
- Everything the banker received equals everything the banker pays out. The app asserts this and shows any mismatch in red.

**What Polo is with money** (2026-10-16, TRUST-12): «Polo solo lleva la cuenta: no cobra, no guarda ni mueve dinero.» (`t.money.disclaimer`) closes Dinero, sits under the TV's Calcutta stakes, and ends the settlement's share card and text; the terms' «El dinero» says it word for word. The labels that read as an institution are a person's or the group's: «Banquero» (the bank's card, «Recibió el banquero», «Paga el banquero», «Vía banquero», «Sin banquero») and «Gastos del grupo» (the house cut, its decisions «A gastos del grupo»); the engine's ids stay (`houseCut`, `banker`, `toHouse`). Whether a free app that tallies private bets falls under the Ley Federal de Juegos y Sorteos is a lawyer's call (handoff 5f).

**Settlement modes**
- **Default, "vía banco":** the banker pays each winner, since money was collected up front.
- **Optional, "sin banco":** a minimized list of peer-to-peer transfers (greedy: the largest debtor pays the largest creditor), for when not everyone paid ahead.
- Both run on **what is still due** (2026-10-01, MONEY-01): flows sharing a payment key form an account (owed, paid, due), and what is marked paid is never asked for again. A vía-banco line names the accounts it closes, and «Marcar pagado» records each one in full (`markPaidWrites`); in sin banco the banker carries the bank's position (the cash he holds, the house cut, anything unassigned). While the tournament runs, «Quién debe qué» is the collection list; once it is final, the settlement is the only list.
- **Every number people pay by is explained on tap** (2026-10-12, MONEY-07; `src/engine/core/money.ts`). Each vía-banco line carries `parts`: every flow still due on the accounts it closes (a flow half paid shows what is left of it, an account paid beyond what it owes shows what is given back), signed from the payer's side, adding up to the line; its `why` lists them and the total, or says nothing changes hands when they cancel out. Sin banco pays out `positions` (each party's parts, the banker's with the bank's in two: what it still collects and still pays); each transfer's `why` is both positions and the share of the payer's debt it carries. `banker.why` says what comes in and goes out by kind and pot, the house's part and what is left. On Dinero each settlement amount, the bank and each «Por juego» amount open their «¿Cómo se calculó?» (`settlementWhy.test.ts` holds the sums on every fixture).

**Por asignar** (2026-10-08, MONEY-05, COPY-09; `src/engine/core/unassigned.ts`)
- **Play is over** when every planned day exists (`rounds.length >= settings.rounds`) and each is finished or cancelled, whatever the tournament's status. From then on the tournament is final for the engine (`tournamentFinal` = Terminado or play over), so what Terminado would pay is what Dinero shows, and Dinero and the «Cerrar torneo» gate read the same flag (`money.unassigned.closing`) and the same buckets: every day rained out lists the whole pot; a day not created yet lists nothing and the gate says to create it.
- Once play is over, money the rules leave with the bank is listed by pot, one bucket per prize-check line: each module on the entries, each instance game (`game:<id>`, on the entries or its side pot), the Calcutta pot (`calcutta`), and entries no prize claims (`pool`). A bucket is what its line budgets minus what its prizes pay; its explanation names the causes (a cancelled day, places nobody fills, a pot nobody won, a contest in dispute) and `contributors` says who paid into it. The keys never depend on amounts.
- **Snake money an unanswered tiebreak holds is not a bucket** (`held`, keyed `snake:<round>:<group>`): it is that group's survivors' as soon as someone answers «¿Quién embocó al último?», so Dinero lists it apart with that answer as the only way out (no «Decidir»), the verdict says «Esperando un desempate» when it is all that is left, and the snake's line lists only the rest. Before round 2 the two shared the key `snake`, so an answer given after the Comité assigned part of the line moved which decision was paid and could pay a group's money twice.
- Dinero shows the list to everyone, each line with «¿Cómo se calculó?» and «El Comité decide»; the verdict reads «Por asignar: $X» (caution) and is red only when the bank does not square with the list. The Comité decides each line: «Dar a…», «Devolver» (pro rata to the contributors, `proRata`: largest remainders, to the peso) or «A la casa», with a reason; «Anular» with a reason.
- Each assignment (one `call_id`) is checked oldest first against what its bucket still holds: one that fits is a prize (`moduleId: 'adjustment'`, labelled «<línea>, asignado por el Comité» or «…, devolución») that every money screen, the settlement, the share text, the ceremony, the CSV and the published results read; the house's part stays with the banker beside the house cut (`banker.toHouse`). One over what is left («Asignación de más») or whose bucket is gone («Asignación sin dinero») moves nothing and is flagged until voided.
- **Terminado while a planned day is still open or was never created** (an older app, a direct update, a restored backup; the gate refuses it otherwise): the modules pay as final, the Comité's decisions keep applying against the same buckets (so what was handed over is never asked back), nothing new is listed (`closing` false, `money.unassigned.openDays` names the days), and Dinero shows «Falta cerrar un día» with which day to finish or cancel instead of a bare «Por repartir» (round 3 of PR 104).
- Dinero also shows, while a day is open again, what the Comité already decided, marked «Se aplica cuando terminen todas las rondas». The «Decidir» sheet reads its line from the live state by key, and before sending fetches the tournament again: a line another Comité phone changed meanwhile is refused («Mientras decidías, cambió…»).
- **«Cerrar torneo»** (`src/engine/close.ts`, `src/components/CloseGate.tsx`): Terminado, «Publicar resultados» and a Ronda rápida's «Terminar y publicar» first check the tournament as it will be once closed and refuse while there are planned days not created, open days, unanswered snake tiebreaks, holes played without putts while a game pays on putts (MONEY-18), money por asignar, unpayable assignments, or unsigned cards of finished rounds (by the tournament's signing rule, UX-23: each pair's under `pairs`, each player's under `marker`, none under `none`). The holes in «Pendientes de revisar» block too (REL-08, 2026-10-09: `rejected_inbox`, refusals of a value a person typed, and untouched defaults refused on a hole nothing else covers; each may be a score that moves money; applying or dismissing them clears them). Conflicts never block (they live on the phone that met them), nor do defaults over a score. Three things only warn: what people still owe (collecting after the trip is normal), lots never auctioned (they cash nothing, MONEY-11; whether they count as self-owned is Diego's open question, so the gate forces no sale), and a server without `rejected_inbox` yet (0028 not applied). The server repeats the part SQL decides without the engine (0029, §7: planned days missing or open, «Pendientes de revisar»), so a stale board, a second phone or an older bundle cannot close those; the rest is checked on the phone only.

### 13b. Course and handicap setup (platform features; first needed for M2)

Three ways to load a course, one place to decide how each player is handicapped. All of it lives in the Comité console and is generic (§0.5): any tournament, any course, any number of tees.

**A. Course search in a golf-course database.**
- A serverless route `api/course-search.ts` proxies a course database so the API key never reaches the browser. Use **GolfCourseAPI** (header `Authorization: Key <GOLFCOURSE_API_KEY>`). Verified live 2026-09-27: `GET /v1/search?search_query=…` returns `{courses:[{id, club_name, course_name, location:{city,country,…}, tees:{male:<count>, female:<count>}}]}` — **tee counts only**; `GET /v1/courses/{id}` returns the full card: per tee `tee_name`, `course_rating`, `slope_rating`, `par_total`, `total_yards` and `holes[]` with `par`, `yardage`, `handicap` (stroke index), split into `tees.male` / `tees.female`. So the UI searches, then fetches the detail for the picked course. Coverage is uneven: **Quivira Golf Club is there (`mz9gqcpj`, 5 men's tees: Black 74.1/142, Gold 72/137, Blue 69.4/131, White 67/120, Red 63.7/107, all par 72); Solmar Golf Links is not** — it's the photo or manual path.
- **Second provider: OpenGolfAPI** (`https://api.opengolfapi.org`, header `Authorization: Bearer <OPENGOLF_API_KEY>`; reads also work keyless, the key raises limits). Verified live 2026-09-27: `GET /v1/courses/search?q=…` → `{courses:[{id, name, course_name, city, latitude, longitude, par, website, phone}], total}`; `GET /api/v1/courses/{id}` → full detail with `tees[]` (`tee_key`, `tee_name`, `tee_color`, `gender`, `course_rating`, `slope`, `par`, `yardage`) and `holes_data[]` (`number`, `par`, `handicap_index` = stroke index, `yardages` keyed by tee color). ~16,800 courses. Data is **ODbL**: when a course is imported from it, store and show the `_attribution` string ("© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL 1.0) via OpenGolfAPI") on the course screen. Coverage for our trip: **Solmar Golf Links is listed (`79fb43c9-bd7a-41f1-8c73-ada89472ad9c`) but with no tees or holes**; Quivira is listed (`7166efab-063c-483d-a47d-3d8f82291664`) also without a card. So for this tournament GolfCourseAPI covers Quivira and Solmar still needs the photo.
- **Search both, merge, and be honest about coverage.** `api/course-search.ts` queries both providers in parallel (each behind its own adapter in `src/lib/courseProviders/`; either missing key or failing provider just drops out), de-duplicates by name + distance (< 2 km), prefers the result that has holes, and marks each result **"Tarjeta completa"** or **"Solo ubicación — sube la foto de la tarjeta"**. Picking a location-only result still creates the course (name, city, coordinates, website) and opens the photo upload right there, so the database and the photo path meet in one flow. Adapter pattern: `src/lib/courseProviders/<provider>.ts` mapping the provider's shape to our `Course/Tee/Hole` model, so the provider can change without touching the UI.
- UI: "Buscar campo" → type a name ("Quivira") → pick a result → pick which tees to import → the course lands in `courses`/`tees`/`holes` with `source: 'golfcourseapi'`, `external_id`, and `imported_at`. Show what was imported and let the organizer edit any cell. Missing key or provider down → the button explains "Búsqueda no disponible; sube una foto o captúralo a mano" instead of a dead button. The route refuses (400) a search under 2 or over 100 letters and a reference over 120 characters before any provider is asked, and a provider whose answer can't be read (`courses` not a list, a hit the adapter can't map) drops out like one that is down (QA-14).

**B. Scorecard photo → course.**
- "Subir tarjeta" accepts a photo or PDF of a scorecard (the same downscale/HEIC pipeline as attachments, flattened on white and sent as a JPEG, §7 «Images before upload»). A serverless route `api/scorecard-extract.ts` sends the image to Claude (`@anthropic-ai/sdk`, model **`claude-haiku-4-5`** — Diego's call: the cheapest model that reads a scorecard well, ~$0.01 per card; if a real card comes back wrong twice, raise it to `claude-sonnet-5` in one constant, not per call, the image as a base64 `image` block, structured JSON via `output_config.format` with a JSON schema for `{ courseName, tees: [{ name, color, rating?, slope?, holes: [{ number, par, strokeIndex, yards? }] }] }`) and returns the parsed card plus a `confidence` per tee. The key is `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on Vercel only (workspace-scoped, no extra header needed). Verified 2026-09-27: Haiku read an 18-hole test card with 18/18 par and stroke index correct, rating and slope included, ~1,000 input / ~300 output tokens ≈ $0.0025 per card. Without the key the route answers 503 and the UI says "Lectura de tarjeta pendiente". A body that is not JSON, or an image that is not base64 text, is a 400 before anything is sent (QA-14); an answer the SDK can't read as the card (a refusal, a read cut short at `max_tokens`, a hole outside the schema) is 422 «No pude leer esa tarjeta»; Anthropic's rate limit is 429, any other API failure 502 with its status (none when the connection never answered).
- The result opens in the course editor **as a draft** ("Revisar antes de guardar"): every hole is editable, the route flags any tee whose pars don't sum to the printed total or whose stroke indexes aren't a permutation of 1–18, and the organizer confirms. Never save straight from the photo.
- Keep the photo as a `documents` row attached to the course (kind `scorecard`) so the Comité can re-check it later.

**C. Manual entry** stays: bulk paste from a scorecard (already in §13) and per-hole editing. All three paths produce the same rows.


**D. Tees per player, per round.**
- `round_tees` (round_id, player_id, tee_id) — which tee each player plays in each round; default comes from `players.default_tee_id` and the round's course. The Tarjeta screen shows each player's par/SI from *his* tee (a par-5 for the back tees may be a par-4 up front).
- Course handicap follows WHS: `courseHcp = round(index × slope / 113 + (rating − par))`, computed per round from the player's tee. When players in one group play different tees, the `(rating − par)` term is what keeps it fair; show it in "¿Cómo se calculó?".
- Then the tournament rules apply on top: playing handicap = `roundHalfUp(allowance × min(courseHcp, cap))`, then the Day-2 cut (§5.2). So the first tournament's "base handicap" becomes: `handicap_index` (or an estimate, below) → course handicap for that day's tee → allowance → cap → cut. The Comité can still override any player's base handicap by hand (`handicap_source = 'manual'`); in that case the manual number is used as the course handicap unchanged, which is how the first tournament's rules sheet read it.

**E. Players without a handicap index: estimate from three scores.**
- Per player, `handicap_source` ∈ `index | estimate | manual`. For `estimate`, the organizer enters three gross 18-hole scores: **buen día, día normal, mal día**, and for each (optional) the tee's rating/slope and par where they were shot (defaults: rating = par, slope = 113, par 72 → "asumido", shown as such).
- Differential per score: `d = (gross − rating) × 113 / slope` (the WHS score differential).
- Estimated index: `index ≈ 0.45·d_good + 0.40·d_avg + 0.15·d_bad`, rounded to 1 decimal, capped at `handicap.cap`. Rationale: a WHS index is the average of the best 8 of the last 20 differentials, which sits between a golfer's good and typical days; the weights encode that (a "bad day" mostly confirms the spread). The weights live in settings (`handicap.estimateWeights`), and the UI shows the three differentials and the weighted result under "¿Cómo se calculó?", labelled **"estimado"** everywhere the handicap appears (badge on the leaderboard row and the player sheet) until the Comité confirms or overrides it.
- Sanity rails: any score below par − 5 or above par + 60 asks "¿Seguro?"; if `d_good > d_avg` or `d_avg > d_bad` (scores entered in the wrong order) sort them silently and say so.
- Engine tests (add to §6): `(75, 82, 90)` on rating 72.0 / slope 113 → differentials 3.0 / 10.0 / 18.0 → index 8.05 → **8.1**; the same scores on rating 71.2 / slope 128 → 3.35 / 9.53 / 16.60 → 7.81 → **7.8**; `(98, 105, 115)` par 72 defaults → 26.0 / 33.0 / 43.0 → 31.35 → **31.4**; `(120, 130, 140)` → capped at 54; course handicap: index 8.1, slope 128, rating 71.2, par 72 → `8.1 × 128/113 + (71.2 − 72) = 8.37` → **8**; index 20 on slope 113 rating 72 par 72 → **20**.

**F. The catalog of Mexico's courses** (2026-10-16, STRAT-14; `docs/strategy/course-catalog.md`). `scripts/seed-courses.mjs` reads `docs/strategy/courses-mx.json` (75 courses by the plan's regions, names and cities only), asks both providers what `api/course-search.ts` asks (`src/lib/courseProviders/endpoints.ts`, the same adapters), and imports only complete cards: each tee kept has par and stroke index on all 18 holes as the provider gave them (`ProviderTee.assumed`: an adapter filled none in), a rating and a slope; shorter tees are left out and named, a course with none is reported missing for a photo. One call per course, `catalog_import_course(p)` (0075, the service role only: refuses an incomplete card whole, and answers `exists` for a course the catalog has by `course_dupe_key`/`course_keys_match` or the same provider id, untouched). Since 2026-10-17 (migration 0077) a name match is that course only when the places don't contradict (the list's city, sent as `city`, else the provider's location, against the catalog's `location`: when both are known one holds the other, lower case and accents off; `samePlace` in the script), so «Hacienda San Gaspar» in Cuernavaca is no longer skipped as «La Hacienda» in Atizapán; a provider id still matches wherever, and `course_keys_match` and the Admin's duplicates are unchanged (tests: `supabase/tests/catalog_import_places.sql`). Dry run by default; `--apply` writes with `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY` to production, or to a local Supabase named with `--url` (any other host is refused; a local target never gets the key from `.env.local`). A provider that answers 429 is asked nothing more that run. The report (`--report`, default `seed-courses-report.json`) lists what it would import, what the catalog has, and what is missing and why. First dry run (2026-10-16, OpenGolfAPI keyless, no GolfCourseAPI key): none of the 75 has a card there, 22 are listed as location only, 53 not found by that name (its keyless search allows 500 requests a day). Tests: `src/lib/seedCourses.test.ts`.

**Where it lands in the milestones:** the engine parts (course handicap, estimate, per-round tees in strokes received) are M1; the two import routes, the review-draft editor and the player handicap form are M2; both keys are on `docs/handoff.md`.

## 17. Testing, rehearsal, and tournament-day runbook

- Gross over par per hole scales with handicap and stroke index, plus noise.
- The chance of a 3-putt rises with handicap.
- Pick-ups happen occasionally.
- It can play in real time (one hole every N seconds per group) so everyone can watch the live boards move during a rehearsal.
- Each player's strokes on each hole are the engine's own (ARCH-16, 2026-10-16): `scripts/simulate.mjs` loads `src/data/testing/simulation.ts` through Vite (`runnerImport`), which maps the rows it read and runs `computeCore`, so his tee, course handicap, allowance, day 2 cut, override and a plus handicap are what the boards will score (it used to work them out in JS, half up and no course handicap). `simulation.test.ts` holds it to the engine on the fixtures.
